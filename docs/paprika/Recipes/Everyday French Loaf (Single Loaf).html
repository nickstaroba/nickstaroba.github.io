Everyday French Loaf (Single Loaf)

Source: kingarthurbaking.com

Ingredients

Preferment

166g Kirkland All-Purpose Flour

166g Water

0.8g saf-instant Yeast


Dough

390g Kirkland All-Purpose Flour

240g Water (110°F)

1.5g saf-instant Yeast

11.3g Salt

Preferment

Pinch Morton Himalayan Pink Salt (optional)

Polenta or Corn Meal (optional)

Directions

Original Recipe

The original recipe is for two small loaves. It has some good links and descriptions of techniques that are worth reviewing but are slightly different than what I do.



Tools

- Pyrex mixing bowls. The thick glass insulates well.
- Brotform for a 3 lb loaf (15.5 x 6 x 3.5"). It's a little long for this loaf, but the width is good.
- NerdChef Steel Stone. This is just the right size for this single loaf or the two loaves of the original recipe.
- Wood Cutting Board (24 x 18"). While shaping the dough, I let it rest on this rather than on a cold surface like stone countertops.
- Reusable Bread Bag. Bread ages better on the countertop in one of these bags as opposed to a sealed plastic container.
- Kitchen Thermometer (probe)
- Baker's Peel (for placing into oven)
- Flour Wand (for dusting flour)
- Bread Lame (for scoring the dough)
- Bench Knife
- Silicon Spatula
- Cotton kitchen towels or linen napkins
- Small mesh strainer



Techniques

- Bowl Fold
- Coil Fold
- Preshaping. I don't do this exactly but I can't find a video to show what I do. I've described what I do below.



Ingredients

- Kirkland All-Purpose flour has enough protein for breads. I like the texture and flavor of this better than King Arthur Bread Flour.
- Saf-instant yeast has been working well for me. I'm not sure how well other yeasts perform.
- Morton Pink Himalayan Salt sprinkled on top of the dough before it goes into the oven for a little salt flavor on the top crust. Other salts turn into black, moist spots on the bread after a day or two.



Timing

This is a two-day process.

The preferment can be used between 12 and 16 hours after It is mixed.

The rest of the process takes roughly 5 hours.

I will start my preferment at 9 PM and set a timer for 16 hours.

The next day, I will start the dough around 12-1 PM to have the bread out of the oven and cooled for dinner.



Day 1 - Preferment

1. In a medium bowl, combine the flour, yeast, and water.

2. Mix until no dry patches of flour remain.

3. Scrape down the sides of the bowl, cover, and let it rest overnight at room temperature, 12 to 16 hours.



Day 2 - Dough

Mixing

1. Measure out the flour, yeast, and salt.

2. Mix the salt and yeast into the flour with a whisk.

3. Warm a large glass mixing bowl with hot water from the sink.

4. Heat water for dough to 115°F in a kettle or saucepan.

5. Empty the mixing bowl and fill it with the heated water.

6. Let the water cool to 110, and then scrape all of the preferment into the large mixing bowl.

7. With a clean hand, use a claw shape to mix the preferment into the water. The mix will resemble chunky pancake batter.

8. Add flour, salt, yeast mixture to the preferment mixture and mix until no dry patches of flour remain.

9. With a wet hand, work the dough for 30 seconds to a minute to smooth it out a little. This step is not crucial.

10. Cover the bowl.



Bulk Ferment / Folding

1. Set a timer for 2 hours. This is timing for bulk fermentation

2. Set a timer for 20-25 minutes. This is timing for bowl and coil folds.

3. After the first 20-25 minutes, with a wet hand do a set of 8-12 bowl folds. Stop when the dough starts to resist stretching.

4. Set a timer for 20-25 minutes.

5. With a wet hand do a set of 6-8 bowl folds. Stop when the dough starts to resist stretching.

6. Set a timer for 20-25 minutes.

7. With wet hands do 2 coil folds.

8. Cover and let the 2 hour bulk ferment finish (there should be about 30-60 minutes left on the 2 hour timer at this point).



Preshaping

1. Dust the cutting board with flour.

2. Use a spatula or scraper to gently ease the dough out of the bowl and onto the cutting board.

3. With wet hands, gently preshape the dough into a round.

4. There are many ways to do this. I haven't found any videos to show how I do this.

5. With the dough on the board, it will be big, round, and flat. With wet hands, I grab the sides and bring them to the middle to start forming a ball.

6. I then pick up the dough in my left and continue to bring in the sides with my right hand to form the ball. Then I kind of twist the connecting point against my palm like I'm closing a jar.

7. I place this seam side down on a floured part of the cutting board.

8. Clean up the flour around the round, and cover with a damp towel.

9. Rest for 20 minutes.



Shaping

1. Prepare the brotform with a liner or towel and dust with flour.

2. Dust the surface next to the round.

3. Flip the round and gently pat into an oval shape with the long side facing towards you.

4. Fold the short sides into the middle of the oval.

5. Fold the near corners into the middle.

6. Roll the dough away from you while applying pressure to create a tube shape.

7. With the seam down, fix the ends of the tube by stretching the dough over and under and let rest on the board for a minute or two to let the seams bind.

8. With the bench knife or your hands, transfer the loaf into the brotform with seam side up.

9. Fix any separation of the seams at this time.

10. Cover with a damp towel and let proof until a floured finger poke stays poked (about 1.5 - 2 hours). This can take longer, but using a bread proofing box or an oven with a proofing mode helps.



Baking

1. Preheat the oven to 450°F for about 45 minutes before baking with the baking steel in the center and rimmed baking pan underneath.

2. Once the poke test works, prepare to bake.

3. Set 1 - 1.5 cups of water to boil. Set oven mitts and a glass measuring cup with pour spout near the oven.

4. Cut a piece of parchment paper.

5. Set out bread lame.

6. Dust the parchment paper with flour or, using a small mesh strainer, sprinkle polenta or corn meal in an oval shape on the parchment paper.

7. Tip the loaf out of the brotform onto the dusted parchment paper.

8. Score the bread in one stroke.

9. Sprinkle the score and top of the loaf with the pink Himalayan salt.

10. Transfer loaf with the parchment paper onto the baking steel in the oven.

11. Using oven mitts and the glass measuring cup, pour 1 - 1.5 cups of boiling water onto the rimmed pan in the oven and shut the oven door quickly to trap the steam.

12. If the bread does not brown evenly, turn it once halfway through the bake.

13. Bake for 30 - 32 minutes or until internal temps are between 190 - 210°F.

14. Place on a cooling rack for 30 - 60 minutes before cutting. Cutting early seems to reduce the lifespan of the bread on the countertop. I store the bread in a bread bag, with the cut side placed down against the counter. The cut side gets less stale this way. Perfectly sealed bags or containers ruin the crust in my opinion.
